# Paper audit

Two kinds of finding, kept apart: **A-xx** arithmetic checks of the published article against its own numbers (generated by `iiot-qelm audit`, needs no data, never corrects a printed value), and **U-xx** observations about the authors' released code at the pinned upstream revision (docs/UPSTREAM.md). Nothing below is a measurement on Edge-IIoTset; the outcome label of every A-line is `recomputed_from_printed_aggregates`.

Version of record: Discover Artificial Intelligence 6:1258 (2026), https://doi.org/10.1007/s44163-026-01704-3 (received 20 April, accepted 22 June, published 6 July 2026).

## How to read the status column

* **consistent**: the printed numbers agree with each other to rounding.
* **inconsistent**: they do not; both printed values are preserved and the origin is left unresolved unless the text explains it.
* **reproducible_from_printed_runs**: a printed statistic is reproduced exactly from other printed numbers. This shows arithmetic consistency, **not** a new model run.

## Text-level inconsistencies inside the article

* Contribution 5 states Friedman chi2 = 54.98 (p < 0.0001) and Cohen's d between 1.87 and 14.38; Section 3.7 / Table 10 / the Conclusion state chi2 = 50.00 and d between 4.99 and 66.46 (A-11, A-12). For 5 seeds x 11 models the Friedman statistic cannot exceed 50.00.
* Section 3.3 says the proposed model is best on 14 of 15 classes (true by the printed table) but Section 3.1's claim that its train-test gap is the smallest among deep models is not (A-14).
* Section 2.3 refers to "Sect. 4.2", "Sect. 4.4" and "Sect. 2.4.4"; the article's numbering changed in revision (cosmetic).
* The abstract and Section 3.8 give 0.9421 vs 0.8764 for MITM; that is consistent with Table 8 (A-09, A-13).

## Statistical caveats

Five seeds on one dataset and one outer split quantify initialisation/shuffle variability only. Seed runs on identical records are dependent, so the p-values in Table 10 are a sensitivity analysis, not evidence of generalisation. Cohen's d with a pooled SD over n = 5 per model is very large whenever seed variance is small; it should not be read as a population effect size.
